# NES Harbor Overworld Layout

The art and the layout constants are generated by `tools/make_overworld.py`
into `assets/overworld/` (`harbor_a.chr`, `harbor_b.chr`, `overworld.inc`).
The code that draws them is `nes/harbor_view.inc` (port card, map, ships,
status bar, name tag) and `nes/harbor_card.inc` (ship card).

## Pattern tables (CHR bank 1)

- `$0000` (table A): the picker's font, unchanged from NES Radar's
  `ldv_bg.chr` (tiles `$00-$53` and `$EF-$FF`), the card frame and the
  pictures of the port card and the four ship cards. The picker, the port
  card and the ship card show their background from here.
- `$1000` (table B): the boat sprites (8x16 pairs: eight headings, eight
  wakes, the cursor), the map's tiles, and the status bar's font on black.
  The map screen shows its background from here (`ppu_bg`).

## The map screen

- Rows 0-23: the map, one tile a cell of HARBOR's 36x36 mask, cells
  (2..33, 6..29). A scope point is two pixels: a ship at scope (X, Y) is
  centred on screen (2X - 16, 2Y - 48).
- Rows 2-5, columns 14-31: the name tag (attributes: white over yellow).
- Rows 24-29: the status bar.

Map tiles are chosen per cell by its four neighbours: `OW_LAND + water
sides`, `OW_SURF + land sides` (`OW_SURF + 0` for land only at a corner),
and open water `OW_SEA` or a wave (`OW_FLECK_A`, `OW_FLECK_B`). A 16x16
block with land takes palette 0 (sea, grass, rock, surf), else palette 1,
whose colours 1 and 2 cycle to make the waves twinkle. A row is computed
outside vblank and written with the OAM, one row a frame.

## Palettes

Map screen: BG0 land, BG1 open sea, BG2 panel white (and green for LIVE),
BG3 panel yellow (and orange for the meter). Sprites SP0-SP3 are the ship
groups (red, light blue, yellow, white); wakes use SP0's white and the
cursor SP2's yellow.

Cards: BG0 picture above the horizon, BG1 below it (colour 1 is the hull,
set per ship group), BG2 white text, BG3 orange (FONT_BASE) and yellow
(FONT_DIM_BASE) text.

## Sprites and the eight-per-line limit

Each boat is two 8x16 sprites; a wake is one more and the cursor two. The
OAM is rebuilt every frame: the cursor and the selected boat first, then
the other boats starting from `oam_rotation`, then the wakes the same way.
`oam_rotation` advances every frame, so where more than eight sprites
share a scanline the hardware drops different ones each frame (flicker)
and the selected ship never drops.
